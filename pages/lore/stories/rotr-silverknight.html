Relics of the Realms - The Silver Knight

The City of Candles

Under the unforgiving gaze of seven suns, and the unrelenting laughter of seven moons, the knight finally came to the end of his arduous journey across the argent desert to the forgotten fortress city of Kal'Dor, the destination of this expedition.

The giant gates into Kal'Dor, the City of Candles, were wide open, untouched for almost two thousand years, or so they say. It is believed the cold embrace of death came to this city shortly after King Calat'on mounted his Great Expedition across the Mortal Realms.

Few escaped to tell of the doom that befell the city, and records differ greatly. However, it is agreed that those who didn't leave the city perished, leaving behind the relics of their lives to the mercy of time, the windblown silver sands of the argent desert, and the foolhardy thieves and raiders who dare enter this cursed city.

The knight slowly wandered the streets of the city, through the empty markets where fine silks, spices and wines were once sold. The alehouses that used to be full of life and joy now stood hollow and cold. The streets were foreboding; only the shadows of the sand-washed towers were left patrolling, keeping a cold grip on whatever flora had made its home here, crawling up the once alabaster bricks, slowly claiming the city as its own private oasis.

As he made his way through the empty streets, he couldn't help but feel the melancholy born from the absence of life and a creeping spectre of dread that followed him. The streets, the houses, the inns, and the brothels were left as if they were in use only yesterday, but the spectre of death loomed behind every closed door, in every shadow, and around every corner.

The knight stopped short of kicking a child's toy, a wooden horse made from the wood of a Lambent Tree. He bent down to pick it up; the ever-enduring wood was still as solid and as pale as if it were newly crafted. He looked around; the maps he had seen may be more accurate than he originally gave them credit for, for he wasn't too far from the square in the middle of the city, where he was sure he would find what he had been sent to search for.

But first he must deal with whoever, or whatever, was following him.

The old man

The steps were quiet but heavy and the breath was like the roar of a lion lost in the desert winds, loud, but not quite there.

His hand found the hilt of his sword, and in one fluid motion he turned, threw the toy horse at whatever stalked him and drew his sword, igniting it as he plunged it into the chest of a Cryptflayer.

The beast burst into flame before falling to the stone paving as dust.

Knowing they hunt in packs and attack as one, he was aware the danger was still his companion.

A wailing scream came from above.

As the knight looked up, he saw another Cryptflayer leap from the ramparts above onto him, but his reflexes were quicker than that and he plunged his burning sword into the belly of the beast, who, burst into flame before falling in a shower of dust.

The knight coughed as the dust covered his face and momentarily blinded him. Before he could recover a third Cryptflayer leaped at him, pushing him down to the stone floor and began to tear at his armour with a savage rage akin to the Roaming Raptors of Ghur.

Within seconds, his pauldron was ripped free and the Cryptflayer sank his teeth into the flesh once hidden beneath. The Knight let out a harrowing cry, quickly drowned out by a short cry from the Cryptflayer. It burst into flame before falling as dust like his brethren before him.

The knight looked up. A gnarled old man stood above him holding his burning sword with one hand and holding the other out in friendship.

“You're lucky there were only three, otherwise I don't know if I would have been much help” said the old man, handing the knight back his doused sword.

“Thank you,” the knight panted, holding his shoulder.

“That will need medicine, come, I'll have it fixed up for you”

“Wait, I”

“No time, before the sun goes down, that wound will have your arm if not your heart, either way that pretty sword you carry would need a new master wouldn't it. Come.”

The old man strode off. The Knight followed him, the pain in his arm building.

Before passing out, he remembered thinking as old and as twisted as the old man looked, there was a sense of foreboding about him, as if he should stand in awe of him. He was also well built for one so old.

Ignis

He woke on a pallet. He felt weak and sick.

“Ah, you're awake. I thought I had lost you for a while”

“Where am…”

“My hut just outside of the city, overlooking it in fact”

“I…”

“No, stay down, you're too weak”

“I need to…”

“Need to what? Find out what happened here almost two thousand years ago? Or find some mystical Realmgate that can open at whatever realm the traveller choses?”

“How do… how do you know…” The knight sat up, against the pressure the old man put on his good shoulder.

“I went through your bag; I wanted to see what you were doing here”

“You what?”

“Oh, calm down and relax, I thought it would be prudent to find out more about the man I have brought into my home to heal”

The knight eased and sat himself up as the old man went over to the kettle hanging over the fireplace and lathed some sort of liquid into a clay cup.

“Here, drink this. Truth is I can help you on your quest”

The knight looked dubiously at the dark thick liquid in the cup.

“If I were to kill you, I would have had an easier time than concocting a poison. It's a broth from my homeland; it'll give you energy to heal.”

The knight took a sip. It was hot and savoury.

“I can tell you much about the Realmgate, but I need a little something from you too” continued the old man.

“Ah, quid pro quo. I see”

“Do I look like an honourable and noble knight who would help a stranger for no return. You're not that naïve” The old man got up and picked up the knight's sword and placed it by the knight.

“That sword is not just a sword. You know this, I think. It has a name too doesn't it”

“Ignis” The knight said, something in the old man's eyes compelled him to talk. He felt, against his better judgement, that the old man were kin to him in some way.

“Ah, so it is, Ignis, the Flame of Old, Shard of Aqshy”

The knight sat up.

“How did you kn…”

The old man pulled two daggers from beneath his cloak.

“Umbra and Sombra, The Unseen, Shards of Ulgu.”

The knight felt at ease at this but couldn't fathom why.

“You look lost my friend. I will explain what I can for my part, but I want to know everything about Ignis and you. I feel we may find our paths may cross more than once in our long journeys.

The Door that Died

The old man scooped the knight another draught of the dark broth and helped him sit up more comfortably.

“What is it you think I'm doing here?” The knight asked.

“You have been sent, my lad, by some of the most slippery vipers in Castelia, the great and powerful Scholars of the Silver Torch perhaps? Or maybe the Priests of Eolas, or maybe even the Triumvirate themselves? Eh?” The old man mocked.

“The Scholars,” said the knight, sickened that he had just tarnished his own honour by telling this stranger who he quested for.

“Have no illusions about any of them lad, they are greedy, self-serving bastards out only for themselves. Vipers. All of them,” the old man continued.

The old man paused for a sip of his broth.

“From what you have in your bag, I would say they have sent you here to see what befell this fair city. Am I right? But I would also hazard a guess that one or maybe two of them may have quietly asked you to seek out a trinket or two, something that may relieve the burden of ignorance about what happened here all those years ago. Maybe they want an inventory of these items, as there may be too many to return with, or even, maybe too big to bring back.

Huh? How does that sound? Am I close?”

“The Realmgate” The knight broke eye contact with the old man and looked into his broth.

“Yes, the Realmgate. I imagine you have been told tales. As most historians, bards, sailors and philosophers have it, almost two thousand years ago Seven Princes, sons of Calat'on, King of Aveinus, were sent forward to explore the Mortal Realms. Does this sound familiar?

They were to embark on expeditions to the realms of Fire, Metal, Shadow, Beast, Life, Death, and even to the Heavens, if the tales be believed. Here they were to build their own colonies, spreading the influence of Aveinus, Kingdom of Illumination.

As this bedtime story goes, it is said that sat at the heart of the city of Kal'Dor, stood a Realmgate like no other. It is a gate that has the ability, for those in the know, of opening a bridge to each and all of the Mortal Realms. In the old tongue it is known as the Llybara Lleuad, or in the common tongue, The Everwhere. How'm I doing so far?” The old man looked the knight square in the face, his eyes burning with a passion ignited from long scattered ashes.

“Yes, I've heard this bedtime story too. Using this Everwhere, it is told that King Calat'on sent forward his armies, led by his sons, on what would be known as the Great Expedition.”

“You have it lad, and I can tell you with all certainty that this is true, all of his sons made it to somewhere on each realm, Firras Firrar in Chamon, The Five Towers of Garra in Ghur, New Albion in Ulgu! All of these were borne of that expedition.

As legend, twisted by time and telling goes, it is this exhibition that opened Kal'Dor up to the malevolent powers that overtook it.

The doom began with Mordrak, The Star Kissed Prince, the youngest son of the king. As it is told, he was the last to embark on his part of the expedition, which would take him to Shyish, realm of the dead.

It is told differently with each teller's tongue, but most will agree that as the young prince approached The Everwhere, hundreds of skeletal hands reached out from the other side and pulled him through, as if sensing his importance, for his retinue of spearmen were allowed to enter before him unmolested.

The Everwhere was left open, its eye firmly set on Shyish for any messenger to bring news back to the king. Three days became five, which became a week. It is told that for nine days and nine nights, the king wept, for he feared his youngest, and what most believe was his favourite son, to be lost.”

“That's one story, but…”

“On the ninth night” the old man interrupted, “the prince returned. His youthful dark skin had turned pale and had tightened unnaturally on his gaunt frame. On this next part, all stories, songs and poems will agree. The prince wasted no time in killing his father, however the way in which he did it may be sung as a simple snapping of the old man's neck, to an epic poem of how the pair dualled a grand melee that left the king bleeding to death in the old stone hall, looking upon the gods of his forefathers.”

“Yes, I've read these stories, I've studies the texts, but…”

“Whatever transpired there, the end is marked by the death of the king by the hand of his youngest son who, as it is commonly told, wielded unnatural powers, killing whatever guard remained with but a sweep of his blade, then forcing the dead before him to rise and serve him once more in eternal service. Many will tell of how his once dark eyes flickered an evil violet glow as he marched through the streets ripping the dead from their crypts. Some say he cut every man, woman and child down with his black blade, adding them to his legions. Others say he killed them merely with a touch, but all will agree that it was he who snuffed out all life in the City of Candles that day.

Once the light of life had been extinguished from the city, it is said he returned through the gate that brought him back to Hysh, never to be seen again, and if it were to be believed, the Everwhere too died, never bridge the Mortal Realms again.”

“So, bedtime stories aside, what you're saying is the Realmgate ‘died'? You want me to go back to Caledonia and tell the scholars that the Realmgate simply died?”

“That's what happened, well only half happened. The Realmgate, by some twist of old magic is still connected to each realm, and can, through the use of some very old and powerful magics be opened from the other end to come here, but no longer the other way round.”

“So, anyone can come here, but none can go out?”

“Exactly, and that's what has been happening for centuries.”

“How could you know this old man?”

“I came through the gate, twenty years ago, though longer it seems, and I have watched others come through. Savages mainly from Ghur I suspect, but who knows. There are skirmishes I hear at night. They don't last very long, clans, tribes and nomads squabbling over territory.”

“Hmmm. So where is the gate now?”

“Thieves and bandits probably took the stone that constructed it decades ago. It has a value to some I imagine, but its physical construct is long gone”

“Then how did you get here?”

“I said the physical construct had gone, but the essence of the gate still remains, roaming this land. You can enter from Aqshy or Ulgu and end up inside a mountain, over a lake, maybe on top of a tribe of Kruleboys. That is up to fate I'm afraid.”

“And why should I believe you?”

“Well, I didn't save your life to lie to you but have it your way. I'll take you down in a day or two, when your fit, and you can see for yourself. All that remains is stone and dust.”

“I can go now”

“Oh, you can go when you please, but I need you at full strength. If we happen upon more Cryptflayers I don't fancy the chances of an old man and a broken knight, do you?

The Eight Shards

“So, whilst we have the time to talk, let's talk about that sword you're carrying” the old man got up and pulled a bottle from his bag. It held a red liquid that he smartly decanted into a pair of clay mugs.

“What is there to discuss, you seem to know more than I it seems”

“I know it is one of the Eight Shards; I once possessed five of them.”

“Eight Shards? What are you on about? There is only one Shard, and that was born in Aqshy, from the breath Dracothian. The story is passed down with the sword from one knight to the next. It is said that during the age of myth the fabled Ice Maiden of Shyish fought alongside Dracothian against the deamon host of Ghul' Duroth. The battle was great, and the losses were dear, but in the end, they held off the forces of chaos. The Ice Maiden was in such awe at the power of Dracothian that she froze a burst of his breath and had it forged into a sword.”

“What a steaming pile of squigshit” the old man cackled a dry rasp.

“How dare…”

“Oh, calm down, it's a great tale I'm sure, complete with noble deeds and songs of the sword and all that, but that is all it is. A tale, designed to hide the true nature of the Shard.”

“If I were…”

“But you're not!” The old man raised his voice. For one so frail, he held power in his voice.

They sat in silence for a moment until the old man got up again and refilled their mugs with some more of the red liquor.

“So, I take it you've not heard of the Eight Shards then?” He said as he returned to his seat.

“No, because my sword is not one of them and I've never come across one”

“As legend has it, the mortal realms were born from one world. This world was held together by eight winds of magic, but it was tarnished, they say, by the taint of chaos and eventually destroyed.

These eight winds of magic somehow created the eight mortal realms, Aqshy, Ulgu, Hysh, Shysh, Chamon, Ghur, Ghyran and Azyr.”

“Yes, I've heard the histories of the Slann Mages”

“So, then you'll know that eventually, early Man, Duardin and Aelf started to be lured over to the ruinous powers. All over the mortal realms, entire clans began to worship the dark gods and turned on their neighbours.”

“Yes, and then came the age of Chaos”

“Not quite! No, no, no, before that, before the Celestial Stormcast Eternals, before the age of Chaos, Sigmar, alongside the Pantheon of Order sent out Eight knights. Some say they were the original Celestials, some say they were Sigmar's sons and daughters, either way, there were eight of them. They were to cleanse the realms of the rising darkness.”

“Where do you get this old man?”

“The writings of Ixtlilton.”

“Pff, some Skink soothsayer who probably found truth at the bottom of a Firewine bottle? Really?” The knight said, shaking his head with a condescending smile.

“You're smiling at me boy, while you're clinging to a desperate tale passed from one brigand to another!”

“Honourable knights!” His smile dropped faster than the head from a defeated foe.

“My boy, I'm sure we can both agree that honour gets one killed. If a knight were to be killed by a brigand or a savage, it would be they who would take the sword, and make up a new story to suit their purposes. Don't be so naïve!”

“Don't pretend to know of my line old man! Continue with your bedtime story while I am still awake”

“So Sigmar sent out these Eight knights, well, this is where it gets confusing, eight went out, but it is believed there were in fact nine of them. I'll explain. He gave each of them a weapon forged from the magics that created each of the realms.

Ignis of the Starclans of Aqshy, his Shard was forged from Emberstone, and I believe is what you carry with you. Then there was Lumis of Hysh, with her Shard forged from Aetherquartz. I had her sword as part of my collection; it was named the Swansword by her people for its shape. Quite exquisite really.

Let's see, ah yes, Mortis of Shyish his shard was forged from refined Grave-sand, then there was the Duradin Tirra and his mighty Warhammer forged from Chamonite. I never laid eyes on it as was stolen from by pirates before I could retrieve it.

Then there was Canis, the warrior princess of the nomadic Ogor Tribes of Ghur. The tales tell of how she carved her Shard from the tooth of the great Mythosaur, whose bones are supposed to be pure Amberbone. I gave this to away to a once very good friend of mine.”

The old man stopped and sipped from his cup; the ghosts of the past seemed to take shape in his mind. The knight felt an odd sensation, as if he could feel what the old man thought. The smell of smoke and fire, of battle, then great loss and emptiness, a hollow that may never be filled.

“Where was I?” The old man suddenly sprung out of his daydream.

“Yes, the Shards, Ignis, Canis…. Ah! Vita! Yes, the great Treelady Vita from Ghyran. Her Shard was quite unique. It was grown rather than forged from the Cyclestone heart beneath her breast. According to the scriptures she would form her hands into throwing axes. When they fatally hit their mark, they would grow life and sprout into a tree.

And Kalla, sweet Kalla, the mute. Scripture says that she was the most noble of these knights. She was to stand guard outside the gates of Azyr to defend the heavens from any sign of the taint of ruination with only her Shard of Celestium.

These realm knights were the finest warriors, poets with swords, wisest of diplomats and powerful sorcerers. They held the threat of Chaos at bay for an age.”

Again, the old man seemed to slip into a sort of trance, remembering something. The knight could feel again, a light, so blinding it hurt. His wince brought the old man to again. For a second the knights vision became devoid of light and was only shadow.

“What about Ulgu?” asked the knight.

“What?”

“Ulgu, you didn't mention Ulgu”

“Oh, Ulgu, ha, well, if legend is to be believed, the knights sent to Ulgu were twins. Umbra and Sombra, each brother was given half a shard, forged from False-stone, two daggers to be precise, and it is here that lay the problem.”

At this the old man pulled his two daggers out to show the knight.

“Sombra decided it wasn't fair that they had to share a realm when the other knights had a realm and the glory all to themselves, you see, I think he saw himself worthy of guarding the gates of Azyr. In some of the scriptures, he resented Kalla for her role, jealous perhaps. Maybe he thought the Pantheon, or Sigmar favoured her, and he hated it.

Either way, whatever scripture you take as canon, legend has it, he left his brother and his realm behind to explore the void. Umbra kept this secret hidden from Sigmar, the Pantheon, and the other knights.

None can say for sure where he went or what he found in the void, but many agree that he returned to Ulgu changed. His shard was changed too, infused with Nullstone.

Many say Umbra welcomed his brothers return, but recognised the change immediately. Ulgu broke out into civil war, the tribes of each brother raging against each other, and years of warmongering, the two brothers fought a great melee, culminating in Sombre slaying Umbra.

Not long after Sombre disappeared again, this time, it is believed he found the Eightpoints, or what would become the Eightpoints. Here he imbued his shard with another Realmstone. Something rare and volatile. Varanite. This weapon was a perversion of the shards Sigmar gave to the realm knights, a mockery of purity and honour.

I think that if Sombre couldn't be the favourite, then he would be the most hated. He wanted nothing more than the full attention of the Pantheon, and if he couldn't have it as their great protector, he would have it by being their conqueror.

Sombra went from realm to realm, killing the other knights, plunging each realm into panic and strife, ripe for the powers of ruination to feed upon. Eventually until he found himself at the gates of Azyr, where he wasted no time in attacking Kalla, who eventually defeated him, but not before Sombra had mortally wounded her.”

“But surely if these knights were Celestials, they would just be called back to Azyr and reforged?”

“Alas, no. Ixtlilton believes it was this loss that may have inspired the reforging process. But all of them were lost, and the shards with them. It is believed the champions of each of the knight's armies may have picked up the Shards to carry on the works of their commanders, but where these weapons went is unknown even to Sigmar himself. You need to remember with the loss of the knights that everything started to crumble very quickly.

The Pantheon broke, mortals began to fight each other and of course, the ruinous powers fuelled this and began to get a foothold in the mortal realms, and so Sigmar closed the gates of heaven. Have you ever seen the Affresco dell'angelo in Opal City. Beautiful piece showing Sigmar carrying the body of the mortaly wounded Kalla through the gates before sealing them.”

“I can't say that I have” said the knight.

“Having said all of that, I managed to gather from various enemies and foolish merchants five of the eight. Umbra and Sombra, as I have shown you, Canis, which I gifted to my strongest ally Vita, Kalla and Mortis. I knew Ignis was here as well as Lumis. That just left Tirra, that sat in the troves of my greatest nemesis. I'm sure he didn't know what he had!

Then my folly brought my world down and I lost it all.”

“What happened?”

“I was ripped here, along with everything else in my…It matters not, it's lost now. All over the desert.”

The old man got up to refill his mug.

A rumble of thunder erupted high above the hut hidden atop a crag at the edge of the desert overlooking Kal'Dor, the City of Candles.

Rain came down. Hard and loud on the wooden roof, but soft on the desert sand.

“That's odd. In thirty years, I've never heard the rain pour over this desert.”

Lightning struck the sand outside, exploding globules of black glass.

“NO!” The old man shouted, getting up from his chair.

“No! No! No! N..”

Lightning burst through the roof, covering the old man and the enfeebled knight in splinters of wood and dried palm leaves. The old man stood, his age seeming to evaporate from him.

“CLAUDICATIS!” Came a voice from the night sky.

Lightning struck again sending the old man reeling backwards.

The knight looked up to see a Celestial Stormcast Eternal clad in obsidian armour. His eyes were as red and as dark as the blood of the fallen.

The old man lay still on the floor of the hut, his eyes wide open, but his breathing was shallow.

“What have you done to him?” The knight shouted over the wind, reaching for Ignis, Flame of Old, Shard of Aqshy.

The Storm

“SILENCE!” Bellowed the black clad Eternal.

He pointed his staff at the old man again.

“SILE PELLEM MUTANTE!”

The old man made no move at all, but lay still.

The storm stopped and moved on as fast as it came.

“Who are you!” The knight stood in his borrowed bed clothes.

“Need you not know knight, but I shall be taking that sword!”

“You shall not!” Swore the knight.

The Eternal struck him, sending him from one side of the room to the other.

Like lightning the pain was gone as fast as it came. He knew from looking upon it that his arm was broken, but he couldn't feel it. He couldn't move at all. His legs, his feet, everything was numb.

“I HAVE THE DAGGERS” Boomed another obsidian clad Eternal.

“AND I HAVE THE SWORD” Boomed the one who struck the knight.

“LET US LEAVE”

And with a blinding flash they left.

***

“Are you alive” The knight asked to the room. He couldn't move at all.

After a moment, the old man sat up, breathing as if he had taken no air for the time he was prone.

“You are! Help me up, I can't move”

“They... they took your sword! They took my daggers! Bastards! “

The old man staggered to where the knight lay.

“Did you bring them, DID YOU?”

“No I didn't! They wanted the weapons. They didn't care if we lived or died, we...we were unarmed” The knight stammered. He stopped and looked at the old man.

“They didn't care if I lived or died, but they put special care in making sure you were disabled, why old man?”

“There are things that are beyond your understanding knight” the old man's focus was everywhere and nowhere. He seemed paniced, but with the focus and searching presence of a predator.

“Then make me understand!”

The old man turned to him, the wild awareness coming to an razor edge point on to the knight who's eyes cringed back at the sight of the old man's yellow eyes.

His nose elongated with his jaw as his old wispy white hair grew longer. Horns protruded from his skull and he grew in stature, his old bones creaking under his tight and thin flesh. Before the knights eyes, the old man grew into a creature larger than one of the Eternals.

The old man's arthritic hands twisted in to wide spreading razor sharp claws. What were once yellowed and rotting teeth grew into fangs.

The creature before the knight folded in on itself, as if it were going to fold into a ball then it stretched out its long limbs and let out an almighty howl to the moon watching silhouetting the creature through the gape that was once the roof of the hut.

“What....who...”

The creature turned to him, fixing him in a deathstare.

“I am the King of a long lost land” the creature spoke in a deep crackling voice, eminating immense power.

“I am the one who survived” he picked up what was left of his torn shawl and wrapped it around his shoulders.

“I will be master of the Realm Shards” he walked slowly upto the prone knight who looked upon him with awe.

“I will be the master of death” and with that, he ripped the knights throat from his body, spraying what was left of the walls with blood that appeared black under the light of the moons.

Chamber Ignotus

“That is all eight?” said the figure standing quite still, his obsidian armour blacker than the starstruck night, his back to his brethren.

“It is. Shall we prepare them for…”

“No. Break them” The figure turned, facing the other two Celestials.

“But Nullus”

“I said break them Stark”

“Nullus, surely we should take them back to Az…”

“We can not risk it Nyx. Bringing them back to Azyr will make it more of a loss if the gates were to fall. We know what is expected of us brothers. We must keep them hidden at all costs; such is the charge of our chamber. Break them, scatter them across the dessert. None dwell here other than carrion and craven, none who are worthy enough to wield them were they to be reforged. Break them, scatter them and we will keep watch.”

The other two looked at the weapons before them, and then each other.

In unison, both Stark and Nyx lifted their Onyx staffs and shattered each weapon in turn with lightning borne from Azyr itself.

“Those blasts would destroy any lesser weapon, you have done well, now scatter them, and stand watch” said Nullus as he picked up some of the shards, and made his way to the East.

Nyx and Stark nodded to one another and made their way Southeast and Northeast respectively.

Under the watchful gaze of the moons of Hysh, they scattered the shards over the argent desert as if they were farmhands scattering the seed into the most barren of lands.
