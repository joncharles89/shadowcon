Encounter at Farpoint Inn

Fifty-one years after the catastrophic event that destroyed New Albion.

Bones

The Inn was full of patrons shouting over one another, singing, drinking, smoking and pinching the backsides of whichever server took their fancy.

The three travellers poured in through the door, bringing with them the winter winds from the Shimersea Bay. Everybody stopped dead to turn and stare at the three travellers. After a moment of utter scrutiny and abject judgement, the music began before the shouting drowned it out.

“I'll get the drinks,” said the short bald traveller whose long and crooked nose led him to the bar.

“I'll find a table,” said the bespectacled one, the snow on his beard quickly melting, putting a cold and drowned look about him.

“And I'll find the piss pot,” said the final traveller, who most certainly shares a branch or two with one of the Ogor family trees.

The only table available sat in the dampest, darkest corner of the inn. It was covered in various layers of drink spillages, some patches being of a moist nature, whereas some were evolving into some very strong adhesive pieces of matter, and of course, on top of it all, the obligatory sprinkling of pipe weed which would inevitably adhere to the clothing of anybody thinking of passing the table.

Oh, and there was a man, possibly a man, could have been an Aelf, perhaps a skinny Orruk. It was difficult to tell as he hadn't removed his hood, and the corner was so dark that his face couldn't be made out when he sipped his ale.

“These seats taken?” asked the bespectacled traveller.

The hooded character kicked out a chair. “Be my guest,” he growled.

The smaller traveller, who had the unfortunate look of a goblin about him, found his way under the haze of smoke from the bar to the table without spilling so much as a drop from the foaming jug of ale.

“You got a jug?” asked the bespectacled one.

“Ay, was cheaper and easier to carry.”

“And saves me going to the bar, cheers lad!” growled the stranger, grabbing the jug and refilling his cup.

“The f...” the bald one spat before being cut off by the Ogoresque traveller.

“What's up?”

“This Squig fiddler thinks he's entitled to some of our brew!”

The Ogoresque traveller sat next to the bespectacled traveller.

“Oh, leave it Gol, he can buy the next round.”

The stranger flipped a gold sovereign onto the table. “I'll buy, you fetch, fair?” said the dark stranger.

“Fair,” said the Ogoresque traveller as he held out a gigantic hand to shake with. “Name's Mo, the ill-favoured looking squib of a git over there is Gol, and this homeless looking pile of shag is Con.”

“Name's Bones,” the stranger said, pulling off his hood (turns out he was a man after all), who proceeded to shake hands with the trio.

“You some sort of doctor?” asked Gol.

The stranger looked at the bald chap sat next to him. “Why would I be a doctor? Do I look like the kind of man who heals the sick?”

“No, well, maybe, I meant on account of your name.”

The stranger just looked at the little man.

“What do you do?” Con slipped himself into the conversation.

“Same as everyone else who's come to Castelia,” said Bones, looking around the bar.

“Passing through?” asked Mo.

“Piss me! I have the disadvantage in life of being the laughingstock of every god in existence.”

“That doesn't sound too good, what misfortune...”

“Because I'm sat at a table with the most ignorant trio in all of bloody Hysh, that's why!” interrupted Bones.

There was a silence before Gol broke it. “So, what do you do?”

“I am an excavator of ancient and priceless artifacts.”

“A grave robber?” asked Mo with wonderous enthusiasm.

“A thief?” asked Gol darkly, his spindly fingers clutching at his coin purse.

“An adventurer!” asked Con, elevating excitement carrying his voice.

“Voices down lads! And don't be too judgemental, you can't steal from what ain't alive!”

“What do you mean 'else who's come to Castelia'?” asked Mo.

“I mean else who's come to Castelia.” With this the old man discreetly gestured around the room. “As in everyone here, and in the next Inn, the one after that, and in the brothel down the way, in the ships flooding up the harbour and in the beds of every hotel from here to Caledonia.” He stopped to look at each of their faces in turn, before dragging a steak-sized hand down his face, stroking his white beard before asking, “Are you lot dense? Or are you as ignorant and as stupid as your little friend here looks?”

“You have us at a loss friend, we're just passing through, not a clue what you're talking about,” offered Con.

“Sigmar save us... A call went out about a year ago, through various legitimate and illegitimate channels, proclaiming that the gates of Caledonia are being opened to the lost and terrible wastes of Aveinus, by order of the emperor himself.”

“What's an Aveinus?” Gol asked, chewing on some sickly-looking toadstools that Mo suspected may have been growing in the damp patch on the wall behind him.

“Nagash take me... we are in Castelia, yes?” Con and Mo nodded.

“And we are the southeastern part of what is known as The Frontier. The Northwestern part is called Aveinus and has been desolate for close to two thousand years now. None go there! Well, almost none, there are wildlings, savages, nomads, barbarians, and maybe an Oruuk here and there.” Bones looked around at his captive audience.

“But!” His fist banged down hard upon the table which made Gol jump out of his chair with a yelp that sounded similar to the noise a dog makes when its tail is stamped on.

“There is treasure out in them wilds boys, make no mistake, there is treasure! Thirty years back, or thereabouts, those Celestial lads from Azyr went out or so I vaguely remember. Black armour they had, black as obsidian! Three of them, Chamber Ignotus they called themselves.”

“You remember wrong me thinks, no such things as Black Stormcast going by the name of Ignotus,” said Mo.

“I saw what I saw, granted I were a lad, but I remember it as if it were yesterday. They went out there, and for days after, Aveinus was covered in storms. We could see them on some nights and hear them on others. They were doing something out there.

And before that, I remember my Da telling me about something he saw when he were a lad, lights over the skies of Aveinus, flickering on and off like the stars had come down from the night sky!”

“Still not seeing the relevance friend,” said Con.

“Then let me tell you a story.”

The Bedtime Story

Bones refilled his cup and took a mighty glug.

“As we all know, and even this vacant looking git knows,” Bones said, pulling Gol in with an arm around his weedy little shoulders, “It was the so called 'Great Pantheon of Order' who brought together the mortals who had survived the great Squigstorm that destroyed the World-That-Was. We built new cities and civilizations, founded countries and gave power to kings, claiming these Mortal Realms for our very own. It was the Golden Age of peace and prosperity. With me so far?”

The travelling trio nodded.

“Good. And I'm sure three fellows as learned as yourselves would know, where prosperity thrives, as does poverty, and thus began the downfall of the age of gold.”

“What do you mean? The Golden Age ended with Chaos taking over?” said Mo.

“No, no, no,” Bones sighed. “The corruptible nature of the mortal races brought the ruinous powers back. The vein, overly ambitious, the weak, the bitter, the downtrodden, any who bared his fellow mortal any ill will gave the ruinous powers a foothold to this world.

These seeds of discord sat quietly in the shadows for years, centuries even, germinating so slowly that nobody knew they had taken grip, until it was too late. Petty warlords began to rise bringing bloodshed and destruction in their wake. Dark sorcerers worked their will, bending crowns and thrones to their twisted designs.

As war began to break out, the Pantheon began to fracture, as we know don't we lads? Eh?”

The trio nodded again, all showing signs of utter uncertainty at why they were being told an odd version of one of the creation tales.

“Bones, no offence, but this is a bedtime stor...” Con was swiftly interrupted.

“But wise Sigmar had a plan. One final plan to keep order over the Mortal Realms,” he said as he grabbed Gol's shoulders again, scaring the small man, causing him to scatter his neat collection of toadstools on the floor, but grasping his attention. His small eyes were fixed onto Bones now.

“Guardians! One for each realm.” Bones sat back, with a slightly knowing smile on his face.

Gol, now free, climbed onto the floor to gather his toadstools back up.

“What do you mean Guardians? The Celestials... what do you call 'em Mo?” said Con as he began clicking his fingers, trying to will his memory to work.

“Oh, Stormcast?” said Mo.

“Ha! No, these came before the Stormers, these were champions sent out to act as the Pantheons sword and shield, defending the realms against the ruinous powers.

A champion! One from each realm was brought forward before the Pantheon to be tested, imbued with great powers and given weapons of the gods. These Guardians were the last defence against the mounting surge of Chaos gathering at the gates of reality, the widening maw threatening to swallow the Mortal Realms.

Did your momma never sing the 'Song of Shards' when she told you this bedtime story?

Aqshy is his Realm, from the Starclans he came.
His Shard forged from Emberstone, scorching with heat,
His legend adorned with adventure and feat.

Beside him stood Lumis, her grace shining bright. Defending her Realm, Hysh, realm of light.

Her shard—shining Aetherquartz, sunrise through elms, known to her people—the Light of the Realms.

Spirits be near him, the whispers of night.

Her Amberbone Shard, that she gave the name “Gnaw,”
From a fiend came the treasure, straight from its maw.

“Well?” asked Bones, looking around at the three.

“No,” said Mo, shaking his head, the corners of his mouth pointing down.

“Can't say that I've heard it,” said Con as he took a glug from his mug of ale.

“Yeah, I've heard it,” said Gol.

“You have?” Mo and Con sang in unison, looking at the ill-favoured creature, making no attempt to hide their astonishment.

“Ay, mam used to sing it to us gobblits when she put us in our cribs at night, or when she told us stories about dad. She said my dad was obsessed with the story, claimed he had one of their weapons before he disappeared. But, yeah, I was brought up on the story of the Eight Shards.”

The Eight Shards

“Well, that does surprise me,” said Bones, turning his seat to face the small bald man.

“Sing your story little man, see if it matches mine.”

And so, Gol began. The others had to hunch over the table to listen to him as he spoke in a mumble mainly into his lap.

“So Sigmar sent out eight Guardians, mighty warriors from each realm—but truth be told, there were nine. See, Ulgu had a pair o' twins: Umbra and Sombra, mirror images of each other. Both chosen, both proud.”

Bones gives a grunt. “Aye, go on, lad.”

“Erm... well, each Guardian got a weapon—a Shard, they called 'em—bits o' the realms themselves. But the twins, they were different. They shared their Shard, made o' Falsestone. That's where it all started to go wrong. Sombra, bitter as the northern wind, thought it unfair they had to share what others had to themselves. He left Umbra alone to defend his Uglu and travelled the void.”

Gol glanced up, checking if Bones were still listening. He was.

“Now, when he came back, Sombra had done something to his Shard, he'd somehow blended it with Nullstone and Varanite. It was a dark thing, and to make it worse, he fought his own brother with it, slew him, and from there, started huntin' down the other Guardians one by one.”

“Right grim tale,” says Bones, nursing his mug.

“Aye,” Gol nods. “Finally, he comes up against Kalla, last of 'em, in her gold armour an' a cloak, white, like Lumis of Hysh lightning itself. They fought fierce and, in the end, she won, but it cost her life. Mortally wounded her y'see. And that were it. The Guardians were gone.” He sipped the dregs of his ale from his cup.

“But surely if they were Sigmars Guardians, they would be pulled back to Azyr and reforged?” Con asked.

“No reforging, no second chances and the Shards, so they say, were left behind. Some say their champions took 'em up... but no one knows where they went. Not even Sigmar.”

Gol pauses. Bones leans in, eyes glinting. “And what d'you know of the Chamber Ignotus?”

“Only what you've said,” Gol replies, just above a whisper.

Chamber Ignotus

“Well,” said Bones, “Actually, I need to bleed the lizard, so to speak. Big lad, ask the barman for his finest Aqshy Single Malt!”

As he walked off, he tossed Mo the gold coin he had placed on the table earlier. It was very sticky.

“Did you say that your dad thought he had one of these 'Shards' Gol?” asked Con.

“Aye, he went off seventy odd years ago to find some of the others, or so me mam says.”

“Seventy... Gol, how old are you?”

“Eighty-one.”

Mo shared a look with Con as he left to go to the bar.

“Which one did he have?” asked Con.

“Which one what?” answered Gol.

“Shard!”

“Oh, mam said he had the Shard of Chamon, a hammer, I think. He said something about some Vampire Duke having them during some war over a hundred years ago. Before that duke became king, he gave a shard to dad, one to his finest warrior—an Ogor that dad despised—and another to his closest friend in some sort of blood pact, I think.

A few years later, it turned out that friend was his mortal enemy in disguise, killed this Vampire King and took the crown for himself.”

“Where was this? Did your dad see the tainted dagger?”

“Erm... New Aveinus or West Albion or something like that. Some backwater place in Ulgu, and I don't know. He disappeared. There was some sort of disaster fifty odd years ago and no one has seen him since. In fact, they all disappeared, along with most of the country.”

Mo returned with a bottle and four relatively clean glasses.

“You two buying any of this?” he asked, pouring them a shot each.

“I do, yeah, but you'll say I'll buy anything,” said Gol.

“It is a bit far though, isn't it?” said Mo.

“It is, but I want to see where it goes, what does a very embellished creation story have to do with a mass migration of treasure hunters?” asked Con.

“Think we're about to find out,” uttered Gol.

“Ah, still here I see. I thought you'd have taken my gold and ran from the crazy man telling stories,” Bones said as he sat.

“Nah, we're baited, I want to hear the end of your bedtime story,” said Mo.

“Right, lets carry on.... So, Chamber Ignotus. Doesn't exist, well it's supposed to be a secret order who has kept out of the way of history since the Age of Chaos. Some of us storyteller types believe that they're a small chamber of Celestials that basically go around the Mortal Realms, bringing back any lost artifacts, or at least keeping their loss quiet. One of their charges, I believe, is the discovery of the Shards.”

“So, let me put the pieces together here Bones, you saw these guys over Aveinus when you were a lad, believed any story that was told to you about them, and think that the Shards are here somewhere because you saw some lights dancing around before some freak storms?” asked Con.

“What? No lad, listen to me. Fifty years ago, as I said to you, Aveinus lit up like never before, sparks igniting then disappearing just as fast. Then, twenty years later these Stormcast fellas turn up. What was it they were looking for? Why were there storms over the planes that have never seen storms before or since? I recon something happened over Aveinus, maybe something or someone came and landed in the desert, and must have been found around twenty years later, hence the Ignotus lads turning up.”

Mo and Con looked at each other with expressions that said quite clearly - 'He's had one too many!'

“Listen, worst case, there's treasure in this waste land from the kingdom that once stood there, but, if what I believe, and what all of these others believe is true, out there in that desert may be the Shards. The weapons that defended the mortal Realms from the Darkness that gnaws away at it.”

“I have one question, actually, I have two,” said Con.

“Go ahead,” said Bones, sitting back.

“Firstly, if these Ignotus chaps found these weapons, would they not just take them to Azyr? And that's if? If they didn't, it's because they aren't there. These Celestials are damn good at what they do, it's not like they would fail to find a weapon in a desert.”

“Well, think about it. These weapons hold the power that, in the right hands, could stem the flow of ruination into the Mortal Realms. If for a second, the minions of Chaos thought these weapons existed and were held up in the vaults of Azyr, it would mean one fatal strike would give total and unconditional control of everything to the Dark Gods forever, but if they were hidden, say in some backwater desert, in the realm of light, where nothing is supposed to be hidden, even if willed by Teclis or Tyrion themselves, then maybe they...”

“Stand a chance of remaining hidden until the opportune time,” finished Mo.

“Exactly,” said Bones, beaming.

“Ok, say you're right, it brings me on to my second question,” said Con.

“Which is?”

“Why does it sound like you're pitching this idea to us as if we're procuring a grave robbing expedition?”

“Would it be so bad? You're going to Caledonia anyway I assume, may as well join with me, keep me safe, and I'll fund a few luxuries on the way, and if I can convince you between here and there to join me, well, off we go.”

“Are you still trying to stitch a crew together Bones?” asked a voice brimming with venom and spite in equal measure. It belonged to a sharp whip of a woman, her dark eyes trying to bore through Bones' eyes into his soul. That was until she saw Gol picking his nose, which broke her resting-bitch-face into a disgusted scowl that would have turned lesser gits to mush.

Lady Thorneycroft

“Gentlemen, may I introduce Lady Thorneycroft, grave robber extraordinaire,” said Bones, eyeing the woman before him.

The trio looked up at the woman, with features so sharp they seemed to guard the cold cruel eyes that looked down upon them, threatening to turn them to stone where they sat.

“Gentlemen, and whatever that is,” she drawled with a suggestive look at Gol before continuing, “I'll give you this one piece of advice, because your misfortunate looks pull at my heartstrings, this man will tempt you with his bedtime stories and promises of fortune and glory, but he suffers delusions of grandeur, poor dear. Has he told you that the so-called Shards of Sigmar are out there somewhere? His little needles in the desert story? has he regaled you with how he saw lights over Aveinus before he went senile?”

As if bewitched, Con stood and pulled up a chair for the lady to join them.

“What's your take? Lady Thorneycroft, is it?” asked Mo.

“There is treasure, of course,” the lady intoned as she poured herself a generous measure from the bottle of single malt. “The Triumvirate has sent out a summons to each of the principalities, giving them licence to equip up to four excavators, treasure hunters, grave robbers, researchers, adventurers or otherwise madmen.” At this she gave a meaningful look to Bones, and a dramatic pause while she sipped her drink.

“With a small retinue, they may pass through the Gates of Caledonia from Castelia over to Aveinus and lay claim to certain surveyed spots that may hold treasures. Be of no illusion gentlemen, there is treasure out there, but this is a land grab. The principalities are sponsoring treasure hunters to find suitable spots for them to lay claim, and an army to cleanse the area of whatever obstructions they may find.”

She sat back in the chair; an icy cold stare aimed at Bones over the glass as she finished her drink.

“Then, my dear Minerva, how do you explain this?” Bones pulled from his robe a scroll, heavy with seals and a thick leather wrap.

~
Esteemed Governors of Castelia,
By the decree of the Triumvirate, Lords of Castelia, you are hereby granted the license to commission four individuals to represent your respective principalities to embark on an extraordinary expedition.
The Gates of Caledonia now stand open, welcoming excavators, treasure hunters, scholars and adventurers, to delve into the enigmatic and desolate lands of Aveinus.
Participants in this bold endeavour shall be entitled to claim and possess any land they discover on behalf of the Principality they represent, free from all forms of taxation.
Additionally, any treasures unearthed during the exploration may be retained by the discoverers themselves.

However, it is imperative that any artifacts, weapons, or fragments thereof made of any Realmstone are promptly returned to the Imperial Palace in Chasmreach. Such items will be rewarded handsomely by the Triumvirate.
Each principality is permitted to equip their selected individuals with an armed retinue, ensuring their protection and success during this perilous venture.
The first of a limited series of expeditions will begin on the Eclipse Mutual at the end of the year.
May fortune favour the brave, and may the legends of Aveinus be illuminated by your noble efforts.

By decree of His Imperial Majesty,
Vaelon IV of House Aevinus,
Sovereign of the Realm,
from the Imperial Palace at Chasmreach
 ~

“Why would they be after anything made of Realmstone?” asked Bones, sitting back in his chair with a wide smile on his face.

“By the grace of Sigmar, save me from the stupidity of men! That's there, my dear Bones, to attract the more gullible 'treasure hunters'. You see those types will more likely give away the shiny things for more time in Aveinus to find these so-called treasures, leaving the actual treasures they have found for the principalities. Idiot.”

Each syllable held a deadly dose of venom, enough to kill a man if the lady deemed it so.

“So, you don't believe?” asked Gol, looking up for the first time at the woman.

“No, but I am not without charity. If you fools want to traverse The Cut and explore Aveinus, then know this. The Eight Principalities have filled their quotas of Treasure hunters already, but Caledonia has one space left. I, of course have secured my spot, but I hear old Korvus has dropped out, ill health, you know how he is. If you hurry, you might just get yourself a spot representing Imperial Caledonia.”

With that she seamlessly stood and strode into the obscurity of the smoke in the direction of the bar.

“D'you hear that, lads? it's just two days ride from Su'An to Chasmreach!” proclaimed Bones.

“What have we got to lose, I'm in,” said Mo.

“Me too, but I want to sleep in a tavern that has a hot bath!” proclaimed Con.

“Would you settle for clean water?” Bones replied.

“Ay,” said Con.
