This is but the beginning (1831)

Merriment at the Grots Head Inn

In a Tavern on the far southern edge of Caledonia, about a week after the culmination of events that would come to be known as the Hunt for the Relics of the Realms...

The Grot's Head Inn was bursting with people from every realm. Orruks drank alongside Aelves whilst the Bonereapers played card games and dice with minor blood daemons, the place was alive with merriment.

The emperor had kept his word and rewarded the treasure hunters and their armies handsomely, and the wastes of the northern country of Aveinus were split out by those who claimed the lands for themselves.

As unnatural as this sight was, the landlord smirked as he watched the only natural thing enter his tavern. Magpie. She was small enough to drift between these mighty warriors unnoticed, which was just as well because on her way out of the dusky evening across the tavern to the bar, she had lifted three purses and was about to pinch a fourth when she noticed the landlord grinning at her.

He towered over her as she approached the bar. He towered over everyone. Some said he was one of them Albino Trolls from Ulgu, others said he was an Ogor, but she believed he was something else as he could count beyond four without overheating and not drink half your pint before he served it to you.

“I'll 'av a pint of HPA please Sal” she said with a wide grin on her mischievous face.

“And who'll be paying for this one?” The landlord asked with a sly smile, eyeing the crowd.

“Oh, that'll the old looking fella by the door, looks a bit green if you ask me” she said with a sideways grin.

“Indeed. Well little Magpie, tell me what you know” he said as he placed her perfectly poured pint in front of her.

“Well, there's a massive land grab over Aveinus, and the emperor's managed to bring together some of those Shards of Sigmar.”

The landlord burst into laughter.

“Has he indeed? Fakes, my dear, fakes. You of all people should know this” he said, shaking his head as he pulled a fresh rag to clean the glasses on the bar.

“I don't think so, I've seen 'em up close on display in the imperial palace”

“What did I tell you about sneaking into the palace, it's not safe”

“I didn't sneak in Sal, I swear! I know people, don't I? Got myself an invite” she said with a smile, this time one of pride.

“I'm sure. So, tell me what you saw.”

“There were bits of some weapons. Not everything was complete, but there was a hammer, a sword, well there were a few swords and a bloody big mace. Let's see, there was an axe, oh, and there was a cleaver, like the one you used to have, remember? The one you told me about when I were a nippa”.

“A cleaver, eh?” The Ogor's interest was beginning to stir.

“Ay, made of bone it were, sharp as anything. They were showing them off weren't they. All of them, dead impressive Sal!”

“Really?” Sal feigned mild interest, but inside something awoke, an old feeling. His gigantic hand twitched slightly, and he dropped the glass he had polished to a high shine.

“You OK Sal?”

“Oh, I'm fine, just clumsy is all, go one little one, tell me more” he said as he shifted the broken shards of glass with his foot.

“Well, as I said, that cleaver was made of bone, which was odd, and the axes were made of wood, even the heads! Can you believe that?”

“These axes… were there any markings on them?”

“Nah, nothing really… oh wait the axe had a rune cut into it, like a G, but some scholar said it was 'hactually an R'” she said, mocking the scholar and laughing into her pint.

Sal laid a piece of parchment down and told her to draw it.

She drew an angular G shape, long and thin.

“It is a G, but it's ancient Ghyran if my memory serves me, and it's pronounced Vegh'Tah. Here and now, we'd say…”

“Vita” finished Magpie, her eyes wide looking at Sal.

Sal leaned close to Magpie, bending over the bar and speaking in hushed tones.

“The daggers, the Ulgulian daggers. Describe them to me?”

“They were so different from each other, one seemed to darken around the edges, but it's supposed to be made of Falsestone, and that's what it does isn't it. But the other… the other looked bent and twisted, like an Orruk made it. It had a cold feel about it, Sal. Real cold. I'd forgotten about it until now, but I remember watching. It was the only one that wasn't shown off by one of the emperor's men. That cardinal chap kept it in a box and took it around the crowd, and after showing it off he took it away.”

She looked sad, her colourful hair seemed to dull, and her shoulders sagged slightly. It was as if the memory of the dagger had taken something from her.

“I… I think I should go Sal, Ma probably needs me, you know what she's…”

“Little Magpie, go home, get some rest”.

“Yeah, yeah I'll do that, bye Sal”.

The Ogor watched his little friend leave the tavern. Then he strode out the back into the kitchen.

He poured himself a double measure of Hyshan brandy, then a second.

He knew that look behind her eyes. That cursed Ulgulian dagger, the one that Sombra perverted with Nullstone and Varanite, supposedly touched by the Ruinous Powers, has a hold on people. For some it draws out rage, or melancholy depending on their constitution. For others, it opens a hollow inside, an ever-hungry maw demanding to be fed knowledge or screams from conquest.

He remembered all those years ago in New Albion, when he stood by someone he considered to be a friend, who was all but consumed by the tempers of that very dagger.

That dagger, those weapons. They had cost him everything.

He sat for a moment, wondering if that wretched Lycan still roamed the wastes of Aveinus, hunting for these shards.

He poured a third brandy and sank it as fast as he poured it.

No matter, he thought, that life was behind him now.

The Man in Black Walks Across the Desert

Whilst the Ogor sat in the back of his tavern, and Castelia celebrated, newly appointed barons planned the rebuilding of Aveinus, but across the great cut that split Castelia from Aveinus, another was making his way into the wastes of Aveinus. His obsidian armour never quite catching in the sunlight under his ragged hood and robes.

Day became night became day again, the sun never really fading and the night never really consuming.

Eventually, he found it. A rune not native to this realm carved into a yellow cliff face. He sat, waiting for the moons of Hysh to rise overhead as violet bled into the azure sky above.

“You have come,” said a voice.

“We have failed. He has the shards, it will not be long before his plan becomes reality,” said the man in obsidian armour.

“Then we must act fast, brother.” The voice became an apparition, a spectre from the past that stood before him.

“My body lies in a chamber inside this cliff face, as do those of all of the other brother spectres that haunt this waste. Remove the Gravesand Stones from our armour so that we may be reforged in Azyr. Our presence here need not be a secret anymore.

We will inform the upper echelons of Ignotus. You will need to send word to our allies. Gather whatever help is available to us brother, for time is short.

I fear this is but the beginning”.
